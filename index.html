Why do we pay taxes?

“Taxes are what we pay for civilized society, including the chance to insure.”
Justice Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr., dissent, 1927 — original opinion

Taxes are required payments to governments. They help pay for shared services and programs that individuals could not easily provide alone. Governments also receive fees, grants and other revenue. People can disagree about how much taxes should be or what to fund; the basic purpose is to finance public functions. The three levels often share responsibility for the same services.

Local · city, county, school district

Property and sales taxes; charges and state/federal aid.

State · Colorado

Income and sales taxes; federal aid and fees.

Federal · United States

Individual income, payroll and corporate taxes; other receipts.

Example: a public school may receive money from local property taxes, state funding and federal grants. Road projects can also combine local, state and federal funds. It is not always one tax for one service.

B · Should I accept a raise? 8 min

Learn · Why some people fear a raise

Imagine a coworker says, “Don't take the promotion! You'll move into a higher tax bracket and bring home less money.” This is a common misconception: they think the higher rate will apply to their entire salary. A progressive income tax works in layers, like filling buckets. Each tax bracket is a range of taxable income taxed at its own rate. When income crosses a threshold, only the dollars above that threshold are taxed at the next rate. Federal brackets alone do not make you lose after-tax income from a raise. Other rules (such as losing benefits) can change the outcome.

Step 1 · Find the income subject to tax

Gross annual wages are earnings before deductions. A standard deduction is an amount the government lets eligible filers subtract before calculating income tax. For the 2026 single adult in our model it is $16,100.

Taxable income = gross annual income − standard deduction (minimum $0)
2026 single filer: portion of taxable incomeRate for that portion
First $12,40010%
Above $12,400 through $50,400 (next $38,000)12%
Above $50,400 through $105,70022%

Source: IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32 (2026 rates and deductions). These are the first three brackets only; higher incomes have additional brackets. The example assumes a single adult who cannot be claimed as a dependent, no credits or other adjustments, and ignores state/payroll taxes.

Worked example · $75,000 salary

First subtract the $16,100 standard deduction: $75,000 − $16,100 = $58,900 taxable income. The deduction is not a 0% tax bracket; those dollars are removed from this federal income tax calculation.

Step 2 · Fill the tax brackets in order

PortionAmount taxedCalculationTax
10%$12,400$12,400 × 0.10$1,240

Step 3 · Add the tax amounts

$1,240 + $4,560 + $1,870 = $7,670 estimated federal income tax.
Income after federal income tax = $75,000 − $7,670 = $67,330.
Effective rate on gross income = $7,670 ÷ $75,000 × 100 ≈ 10.23%.

Even though the highest bracket reached is 22%, only $8,500 is taxed at that rate. Your actual paycheck also includes other taxes and deductions; this is not total take-home pay.

Learn · What does filing taxes mean?

Throughout the year an employee's employer usually sends part of each paycheck to the IRS and Colorado as withholding. At year-end, filing a tax return accounts for taxable income, deductions, credits, tax liability and those payments. It determines whether more tax is due or a refund is available. A refund is not automatically free money: it may be a return of overpayments, refundable credits, or both.

DocumentWhen / purpose
W-4At a new job or when your situation changes: give your employer information to calculate federal withholding. An updated W-4 changes future paycheck withholding, not the tax rules.
W-2After the year ends: employer's report of annual wages and taxes withheld. Use box 1 (federal wages) and box 2 (federal income tax already withheld).
1099A family of forms that may report nonemployee pay, interest or other income. Income can still be reportable without one.
Form 1040Federal individual income tax return: report income, calculate tax, compare with prepayments and credits, then claim refund or pay balance.
Colorado DR 0104Separate Colorado individual income tax return. Prepare federal first, then use it to complete the state return.

When and why should I file?

Tax Day: April 15. The usual deadline to file and pay for the preceding calendar year is April 15 (may move for weekends, holidays or other relief). The 2026 tax year return is due April 15, 2027. An extension to file generally does not extend time to pay. Colorado residents generally must file a state return if required to file a federal return or if they have Colorado income tax liability. You may want to file even if not required, to recover withholding or claim eligible refundable credits.

What if my parent or guardian claims me as a dependent? Being a dependent does not automatically prevent you from filing your own return. Report your own income and indicate you can be claimed, if applicable. Dependents have different filing and deduction rules. With just $6,000 of wages and $180 of federal income tax withheld, a dependent with no federal tax liability may file for a $180 refund. Check both federal and state filing rules with a trusted adult or official source; never enter real family information here.

Real-life problems: what should I do?

I owe taxes but cannot afford to pay.

Suppose Jordan owes $1,132 but has only $400 available. If a return is required, file by the deadline anyway, pay what you reasonably can, and contact the IRS about payment options for the remaining $732. Check Colorado’s payment options separately for state taxes. Interest, penalties and possibly fees can apply; an extension to file is generally not an extension to pay. Do not ignore official notices.

Official IRS payment plans · Colorado Department of Revenue

I have two jobs.

Imagine Jordan works at a warehouse and a restaurant. Each employer may calculate withholding without fully accounting for the other job. Together, the wages can create a larger tax liability than withholding covers. Use pay stubs from both jobs with the IRS withholding estimator and update Form W-4 if needed. Check again when a job or income changes.

A refund is not automatically a bonus. A worker with $1,000 tax owed and $1,300 withheld receives $300 back. Another with the same tax owed and $1,000 withheld receives nothing back. Both paid $1,000 in final federal income tax. A refundable credit is different: it can create a refund beyond prior payments. Even with zero federal income tax liability, Social Security and Medicare payroll taxes may still apply.

D · Am I leaving money on the table? 10 min

Learn · Why do tax credits exist?

Governments use credits to support specific people or activities, such as low-income workers, families with children or education. A tax credit lowers the amount of tax you owe. Unlike a deduction, which reduces the income used to calculate tax, a credit reduces the calculated tax itself. Some credits are nonrefundable (they generally reduce tax only to zero); others are refundable (the unused eligible amount can be paid to you).

Deduction: reduce taxable income

$20,000 income − $1,000 deduction = $19,000 taxable income. If the whole deduction is in a 10% bracket, tax savings = $100.

Credit: reduce tax owed

$1,500 tax owed − $1,000 eligible credit = $500 tax owed. Tax savings = $1,000.

When a refundable credit exceeds the tax

Example: a worker has $200 tax liability before credits, no withholding, and qualifies for a $500 refundable credit.

$200 owed − $500 refundable credit = −$300.
The negative amount means the worker receives a $300 refund (assuming no other tax or payments).

Now you try a different example in the questions below. To find a refund when the refundable credit is larger than tax owed, subtract the tax owed from the credit.

The Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)

This refundable credit is designed to help qualifying low- to moderate-income workers. Eligibility depends on income, age, family circumstances and other requirements. You must file to claim it. IRS estimates for tax year 2022 show about 23% of eligible Colorado taxpayers did not participate. That is why it is worth checking.

Federal EITC rules · Colorado EITC rules · IRS Free File · VITA free tax assistance

Federal childless EITC generally requires age 25–64. Colorado has additional eligibility pathways, including certain workers ages 19–24 who are not specified students, some former foster or homeless youth, and some ITIN filers. In tax year 2026 the Colorado credit is 25% of the applicable federal credit calculation. See the Colorado eligibility details. Other rules apply. Investigate the profiles using official information, not your own private information.

E · My TikTok side hustle 11 min

Learn · A business does not automatically pay your taxes

You can earn income from a job, selling products, mowing lawns, photography, delivery apps, TikTok videos, sponsorships or freelance services. If you are an employee, your employer usually withholds taxes. If you operate a business as a self-employed worker, the customer or platform may pay you without subtracting tax. You are responsible for keeping records and reporting the income, even if no 1099 arrives or you are paid in cash.

W-2 employee

Paycheck → employer withholds taxes → annual W-2 → file return.

Self-employed worker

Receive payment → record revenue and eligible expenses → save for taxes → make estimated payments if required → file return (usually Schedule C and Schedule SE for a sole proprietor).

Business deductions · What can you claim?

Revenue is all money the business receives before costs. An eligible business expense is generally an ordinary (common in your line of work) and necessary (helpful and appropriate) business cost. Keep receipts. Expenses must genuinely relate to the business; personal spending is not automatically deductible. If an item is used for both work and personal life, generally only its eligible business portion can be deducted. Equipment may follow special rules.

ExampleLikely treatment (check actual rules)
Editing software used exclusively for paid creator workMay be an eligible business expense.
Microphone used for videos and personal useOnly eligible business-use portion, subject to equipment rules.
Personal vacation unrelated to creator workPersonal cost, not automatically deductible.
Net business profit = business revenue − eligible business expenses.
Example: $2,000 creator revenue − $500 eligible expenses = $1,500 profit.
A 25% reserve on $1,500 = $375 saved for taxes (illustrative, not your actual tax amount).

Protect your future self

When payment arrives, do not assume it is all available to spend. Track income, move an appropriate portion of profit into separate tax savings, and check whether estimated payments are required. Net earnings from self-employment of $400 or more generally require filing. Federal income tax, state income tax and self-employment tax can apply. Estimated federal payments may be needed if you expect to owe $1,000 or more after withholding and refundable credits; other rules apply. As your business grows, consult a qualified tax professional (such as a CPA or enrolled agent) and compare fees. They can help you understand eligible deductions, recordkeeping and payment requirements.
